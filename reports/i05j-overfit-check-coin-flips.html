An overfit check meets coin-flip trading strategies

Full experiment · SPY hourly data · Two separately selected winners
ELEVEN-YEAR WINNER: PASSED · TWO-YEAR WINNER: FLAGGED

The same check gave different answers to two different search winners. Our eleven-year winner passed the prewritten rule; our separately selected two-year winner was flagged. Both results belong in the conclusion. We did not test the strategy from the source video.

Source video: GPT-6 Astra Built 200 Python Strategies. Here’s the Winner (300%), Algo-trading with Saleh, by Saleh Mir. The percentage in that video title is his reported figure, not ours. He maintains Jesse, makes the tool available, shows losing variations and warns that a check cannot guarantee future returns. We do not know who operated that check, its exact settings or whether other held-out testing happened elsewhere.

We tested an instrument: Jesse’s Monte Carlo candles. It reruns a fixed strategy on rebuilt price histories. It does not repeat the original strategy search inside each rebuilt history. Rebuilt historical prices and data withheld from strategy selection answer different questions.

What the two searches and checks returned

All own strategy returns below are total backtest returns over the stated window, before trading fees, not annual rates or forecasts. They are from the amended design on repaired price data. The cleaning decision followed a diagnostic; it was not pre-registered before that diagnostic. Buy-and-hold is the same fund’s price change, not a risk- or leverage-matched portfolio and not dividend-inclusive total return.

On the eleven-year search, the selected winner returned +322.83% across 353 trades. Across that same 2,048-configuration search, the median was -63.33% and 78.5% lost money. Selection makes this winner exceptional within its own searched history; the random-entry code establishes why its signal contains no price information.

On the later window, 2015-01-01 through 2026-06-22, that same frozen eleven-year winner returned +93.27%, while holding SPY returned +262.1%. It made money, and it earned much less than holding the asset. A later profit does not make the entry signal informed. The return comparison is separate from the Monte Carlo classification.

Controls that can fire, and controls that should stay quiet

On eleven years, full foresight was flagged; 0 of 10 unselected random configurations were flagged. On two years, full foresight was flagged; 0 of 10 unselected configurations were flagged, and 1 was grey. Grey is not a flag. These controls show useful discrimination in these constructions, not universal reliability.

The pre-registered conditional partial-foresight arm ran because the eleven-year winner did not flag. Preset steps overshot its target: on eleven years, partial foresight returned +470.30%, about 1.46 times the eleven-year winner’s return. It also passed on eleven years; the two-year selected coin-flip winner was flagged. We did not retune the dose to an exact match. This rules out a neat claim that the instrument catches foresight but misses selection.

Repairs, deviations and limits

Price repair was adopted after the diagnostic and was not pre-registered before it. The final rule replaced 2 bad closes and 1 bad open, clipped 6 highs and 7 lows, without dropping rows. It uses a ten-percent neighbor/body threshold; the bad-open clause was added after inspecting the raw rows. Such a rule can alter genuine extremes and cannot certify prices correct. The old dirty-data winner supplies no evidence for this episode.

Cross-engine parity then failed structurally: fills at an hour’s close and the next hour’s open can resolve stops/targets differently. The dated third amendment selected afresh inside Jesse and replaced parity with repeatability checks. We do not claim that the two engines agree. The initial wrong-unit schedule and failed/cached partial-foresight attempts were excluded; no preferred failed attempt was selected for publication.

A corrective rerun, not a separately pre-registered arm, repaired the full-foresight construction. The initial eleven-year control kept 93 in-run winning entry hours; the literal judged-alone rule kept 97. They agreed for 349 of 353 trade signs. Both eleven-year controls flagged. The two-year control agreed under both readings. Both versions remain in the arm ledger, with the correction labeled.

The eleven-year selected-winner run returned 187 of 200 requested scenarios; 13 failed scenarios were dropped by the tool. The two-year winner returned 200 of 200. A diagnostic after results, not a pre-registered causal experiment, traced failures to rebuilt paths reaching the price floor and invalid stops. The window and winner changed together; price wander is a clue, not an isolated cause. Dropped-path bounds were later descriptive checks, not a new class rule.

Fixed seeds, reduced control scenario counts, zero fees, long-only entries, hourly SPY prices and leverage conventions are ours. The source prompt included trades in both directions and a slower chart. The source demonstration does not describe held-out data on camera; that observation is not proof that none existed elsewhere. Its strategy’s overfit status remains unknown from this experiment.

  3. A Jesse release changing the check or defaults: rerun the primary test.
