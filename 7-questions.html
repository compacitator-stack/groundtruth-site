The 7 Questions to Ask Before You Trust Any Strategy With Money

From the channel that tests trading and money promises the way a desk would.

1Is the claim in writing?

Exact rules, exact numbers, somewhere public. "My students make 2–3% a week" is a claim; a rule you could hand a stranger and get the same trades is a method. If the rules aren't public, you're not buying a strategy — you're buying a subscription to someone's judgment.

2Who's counting — and are they grading their own homework?

A win rate reported by the person selling the course is an advertisement, not a measurement. Ask: could this number be checked by someone who wants it to be false?

3Where's the graveyard?

For every account that turned $5k into millions, how many blew up quietly? Survivors post screenshots; the graveyard doesn't post at all. Any result that doesn't count the quitters is counting wrong.

4What did it cost to run?

Commissions, spreads, slippage, the fill you actually get at 9:31 — real costs routinely eat strategies that look great at mid-price. If the backtest doesn't name its cost assumptions, the backtest is fiction with extra steps.

5Was the pass/fail line drawn BEFORE the results came in?

Anyone can draw the target around the arrow after it lands. A serious test says "here's what failure looks like" first — and accepts the answer.

6Does it survive the boring math?

Position size × win rate × payoff × how many times you can actually take the trade. Most "$500/day" promises die right here, before any market data is needed.

7What would change their mind?

Ask the seller what evidence would make them retire the strategy. If the answer is nothing — you're not looking at a method, you're looking at a belief. We publish ours with every verdict.
