Riley Coleman's Free 5-Step Checklist — Full Test Report

VERDICT — THE WRITTEN SKELETON IS DEAD (0 of 4 GOALPOSTS)

Riley Coleman's free five-step entry checklist, coded exactly as written and run on 1,212 trades across 22 years of survivorship-free one-minute data, loses money before a single cent of cost is charged — -0.080 R per trade at zero friction. It won 31.3% of its trades against the 33.3% break-even that a 2R target requires. Two points short, for free.

What we granted, and what we actually tested

Granted, not measured: that Riley Coleman trades profitably. We never audited his P&L and this test cannot. He is on record in our own files as a genuine, honest live trader — he narrates real trades, shows losses on camera, and says out loud when months have gone badly. The question here is never the man.

Tested: only the part of the method that fits in a PDF — the written checklist, handed to a machine. One step set of six was mechanized. Four components were deliberately excluded and named in writing before the first bar loaded (§1 below).

What this report does NOT claim, pre-committed before the data: our word for what this test says about Riley Coleman's discretionary method is non-diagnostic. The probe strips out the two things he himself says carry the edge — the big-picture directional read and the in-trade management. A dead skeleton is equally consistent with "his edge lives entirely in the discretion" and with "there is no durable edge." This instrument cannot separate those, and does not try.

§1 · The locked pre-registration

Committed 8a8540e on 2026-06-29, before the probe was run — the wager you cannot walk back once the data arrives.

The falsifiable question

Does a purely mechanical fade of an "unhealthy" opening drive into an early-session extreme — entered on a momentum-reclaim trigger in the 09:40–10:30 ET window, stopped beyond the extreme, with a fixed-R target — produce positive net-of-cost expectancy, robustly across the sample?

  • Yes looks like: net expectancy > 0 after a 0.05R friction haircut, and positive in both sample halves, and not driven solely by the top-5 days, and beating the "fade every drive" benchmark.
  • No looks like: any one of those four failing.
  • Ambiguous residue: gross-positive but net ≈ 0 (friction-bound), or exactly one check failing near threshold.

Our stated prior: ~30%

Written down before results: about a 30% chance the mechanical skeleton clears net-positive robustly. Recorded so that a positive result would have had to overcome a low prior — and so a negative one could not be rationalised away as expected. Grading our own prior: the outcome landed in the 70% we called more likely.

§3 · Was it the path? Three stress tests

  • Split the 22 years in half — both halves negative: -0.181 R (606 trades) and -0.079 R (606 trades). No lucky era.
  • Remove the five best days — it gets worse, -0.138 R, because those extreme days were net contributors against the strategy (-6.2% of net). Not an outlier story.
  • Sweep every setting — nine parameterizations (three drive thresholds × three targets). All nine came back negative at realistic cost. No magic setting.
  • Breadth — the setup found trades in 17 calendar years and never fired at all in 6 of them. Of the 17 it traded, 13 lost money at realistic cost.

§4 · The two controls — and the row that made us sit back

Control B1 — fade every morning drive, with his signature "unhealthy move" filter removed entirely. It traded 4,050 times, more than three times as often, and came out roughly break-even before costs: +0.009 R gross (38.3% win), -0.042 R after realistic cost.

Requiring the big stretched drive — his single most-emphasized signal — makes expectancy worse: -0.080 R against +0.009 R. The mechanized version of the filter selected worse entries, not better.

Read that caveat the right way, because it protects him, not us. The mechanism is unglamorous: big early drives tend to keep going, and sharp bottoms are brutal to time. Riley applies that filter together with the big-picture read we deliberately removed — the judgment about which drives are exhausted. We removed the eye and kept the rule the eye was holding. What died on our desk is the rule without the eye.

Control RC — random timing. Replace the momentum-reclaim trigger with entry at a random qualifying minute: -0.155 R at realistic cost, against the real trigger's -0.130 R. The trigger adds a little. It does not add an edge.

§6 · His own risk-reward terms

Two R was our choice; his framing is a bigger reward-to-risk. So we ran a 3R target, where the naive break-even drops to 25%. Measured win rates at 3R: 25.9%, 26.3%, and 24.9% across the three drive thresholds. Expectancy stays negative anyway (-0.049 R at the primary threshold) — because of the clock. The 12:00 time-stop closes whatever is still open and those exits pay about 1R apiece, so the average winner shrinks to 2.579R and the real break-even moves to about 27.9%. Out of reach from any cell.

An erratum we are publishing rather than quietly fixing. An earlier lab note attributed to Riley the claim that "30–40% win at 1:3 is profitable." That is uncitable — it appears in none of the four primary sources, and it was a lab-time paraphrase. It must not be quoted as his, and it appears nowhere in the episode. What is on tape, verbatim: "with how the risk-reward is… even with a 50% win rate like it'll be, um, extremely extremely profitable." The break-even arithmetic stands on its own and needs no attribution to anyone.
