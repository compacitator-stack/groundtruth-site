Your Backtest Is Missing Dead Companies. We Put Them Back and Measured It.

Episode B01 · report format v2 · lab run 2026-08-15, pre-registration dated 2026-08-15 — not hash-locked
VERDICT — REAL, USUALLY FLATTERING, AND SMALLER THAN WE FIRST SAID

Deleting the companies that died flattered this strategy at 4 of 4 basket widths — by +1.16 to +2.49 CAGR points a year, with a drawdown 4.51 to 6.62 points shallower than the truth. But the effect has no fixed size and not even a fixed direction: on a leveraged, concentrated version of the same strategy it ran backwards, costing 2.68 points. Survivorship bias is real and it is not the reason your backtest and your account disagree.

What we granted, and what we actually tested

Granted, not measured: that survivorship bias exists, that it usually flatters, and that every practitioner warning you about it is right to.

Tested: how much it is worth, in CAGR points and drawdown points, on one strategy we built ourselves and had already believed.

What this report does NOT claim, pre-committed before the data: that this explains the gap between backtests and live accounts; that the direction is a law; that a delist count carries a return effect; or anything at all about membership survivorship, which is a second effect we did not measure.

§1 · The pre-registration — and what it is not

This pre-registration is dated, not hash-locked, and we are saying so. It was written on 2026-08-15, before any code in the lab directory; the inherited arm's was written 2026-08-11 (UTC). But no commit lock exists for either: the pre-registration and the script it governs landed in the same commit in both labs, so version control cannot witness the ordering. What stands behind the dates is a written provenance note, which is an attestation and not cryptographic evidence. Our A13 report could cite a pre-run lock hash; this one cannot, and filling the gap with a hash that does not exist would be the exact move this channel was built to catch.

The falsifiable question

The survivorship verdict-flip vs spy holds at >= 3 of 4 basket widths.

  • A yes looks like: the survivorship-free arm loses to SPY while the survivor-only arm beats it, at 3 or more of the 4 widths — the verdict itself flipping on the data choice alone.
  • A no looks like: the flip appears at fewer than 3 widths, meaning the bias moves the number without moving the verdict.
  • Ambiguous residue: a flip at exactly 2 of 4, which we pre-committed to read as unresolved rather than as support.

Our stated prior: we expected the flip to hold at 3 or more widths

It held at 1 of 4. The prediction was falsified. The bias moved the number at every width and moved the verdict at only one, which is a smaller claim than the one we registered — and the one we are left with.

Deliberately NOT tested — named in advance

  1. Transaction costs and slippage — excluded so the one variable stays one variable; it also means every number here is a gross number.
  2. Membership survivorship — the second effect a real user inherits, unmeasured and not claimed.
  3. Delisting force-exit — a dead name's return goes NaN and is dropped rather than sold at a loss, which flatters the survivorship-FREE arm too.
A flip would have said the data choice decides the verdict; no flip would have said it moves the number only. Both endings were disarmed on paper before the run.

§3 · Was it the path? The stress tests

The sweep across basket widths is the stress test: the same question asked four times at 5, 9, 15, 25 names. The sign of the effect survived all four. Its size did not — it moves by a factor of roughly two across widths, which is why this report gives a range and never a single number.

Both arms reproduced exactly on re-run (CAGR and drawdown deltas both 0.0), in 82.1s of a registered 2700s budget.

§4 · The controls

SPY, identical in all 8 cells: $65,896.78 from $10,000, 9.07%/yr, -56.47% max drawdown. It is the same instrument in every row, so any difference between rows is the basket, not the market.

The ghost bound. Dead names occupy 0.000% to 0.724% of basket-day slots. That is a bound on how much of the effect can be mechanical, not a second measurement of the effect.

§5 · The best objection, pre-registered and answered

The best objection is that our survivorship-free arm is itself flattered. It is. When a held name's data ends, its return goes NaN and is dropped — there is no force-exit at a loss and no cost charged. So the true gap between the two universes is at least what we report, never exactly it. Every delta on this page is a floor.

The second objection is that active tracks symbols, not companies. Also true. A ticker change, a merger and a bankruptcy all read identically in this flag — UTX, which merged, sits beside LEH, which did not. So the honest phrase is symbols that stopped, not companies that went to zero.

§8 · What would change our mind

  1. Send us a strategy where putting the dead companies back improves it on both return and drawdown, and we will run it here. We already found one ourselves — the leveraged arm above — which is exactly why we will not pretend the direction is a law. Find us a second and this becomes a sequel, not a correction.
  2. Show that the untyped bucket is not what we assume. Our census floor rests on 6,768 rows that carry no type and of which 0 are active. If a large share of those are not common stocks, the floor moves and we will move it in public.
  3. Publish a per-trade reconstruction that contradicts the grid. We report endpoints because that is what the simulator returns; a fill-level run that disagrees with these eight cells would be a finding and we would rather have it than not.

§9 · What survives

Survivorship bias is real, and it is smaller than its reputation. On this strategy it is worth +1.16 to +2.49 CAGR points a year — enough to matter, nowhere near enough to explain why a backtest and a live account disagree.

The direction is not a law. We found a case where it reverses, on a leveraged version of our own strategy. Anyone quoting you a single number for survivorship bias is quoting a number that depends on what they were running.

And the check is cheap. Ask what list the backtest was allowed to buy from. If the answer is today's list, you already know which way the number leans.
