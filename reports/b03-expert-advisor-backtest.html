Can you trust an expert advisor’s backtest? The full report

Own procedure · US stocks · Full test report
REGISTERED TEST: INCONCLUSIVE

Our first prediction required positive median fitting-window alpha over holding the same stock. The measured median was -5.59 percentage points; 50 of 106 selected settings beat that benchmark. The plan’s own rule therefore makes the registered test inconclusive. The other measurements remain descriptions, not a registered pass or a verdict on a product.

An expert advisor is a trading robot; a backtest simulates a rule on historical prices. We tested the procedure of choosing a historical winning setting, then freezing it for later prices. No commercial expert advisor, vendor product or set file was tested. A backtest may accurately describe its fitting years while leaving later performance unanswered.

Returns compound completed trades over the whole requested window, not per year. Positions still open at the end are excluded. The headline figures are before trading costs. Buy-and-hold uses the stock’s last closing price in each window, so it treats the endpoint differently and is not matched to every risk characteristic of the strategy.

Costs, missing histories and checks added later

The typical-setting fitting return falls from median 7.05% before costs to 5.01% with 0.05 of initial risk charged per completed round trip. This is the typical-setting slice, not the selected-winner headline.

The daily store includes delisted names on their historical dates, and the universe was selected as it stood at the start. That avoids selecting only present-day survivors, but ticker strings do not reliably track an instrument through renames or reuse. Of 60 selected symbols, 53 qualified. Positions left open — not pre-registered

In a check that was not pre-registered, 41 of 106 fitting windows ended with a position open at the selected setting; 17 of 106 later windows did so in that check that was not pre-registered.

In a check that was not pre-registered, valuing those positions at the last close, without cost and without reselecting settings, moved median fitting alpha from -5.59 to -5.95 percentage points. Both are negative. No registered figure or grade changes.

This later check used one convention: last close, no cost, no next-open exit. It re-read only the sixteen headline settings, not the wider grid, search curve, spike/plateau statistics or friction cells. Joined histories remain included. A last stored close may precede the calendar window’s end, and sale at that price was not verified. It does not establish a full account-value simulation or the behavior of a rule that closes positions at the endpoint.

In that check that was not pre-registered, 8 of the 17 later windows and 1 of the 41 fitting windows ending open had price histories that stopped before the requested period ended; holding lengths count bars, not calendar days.
