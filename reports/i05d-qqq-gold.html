§5 · The strongest objection and the implementation limits

The strongest objection is that these are selected historical dates. A once-only release stops additional tuning by this analyst; it does not make the creator’s already-selected history independent. Both long windows overlap, all fixed settings are correlated, and the short later periods are especially fragile.

Daily vendor opens are fill references, not proven auction fills at real account size. GLD is our stand-in for gold. Costs are modelled, not a broker-fill audit. We use a simple price average, fractional shares, zero cash interest, initial purchase and scheduled final liquidation. Dividends accrue to prior-close holders on ex-date, become cash at payment-date close, and reinvest at the next open with cost. Unrealized receivables are valued at face value. Taxes and leverage are excluded.

The model counts reinvested distributions separately from split-adjusted prices. Two QQQ quarters in 2005 and one SPY payment date in 2006 remain uncertain: all eight registered combinations are bounds, not recovered facts. No result was chosen from a preferred corner. The full-history primary ranges shown above retain all corners.

§8 · What would change our mind

  1. Exact dates and return conventions. Supply the source window and what the portfolio percentage measures; register that literal comparison before its outcomes.
  2. Complete source rules and records. Supply funnel floors, candidate catalogue, cohort mapping, sleeve and portfolio rules and ledgers; missing specifications are not replaced with a convenient demonstration.
  3. Corrected evidence or implementation defect. A documented bar, corporate-action or code error reopens affected cells with the original evidence preserved.
  4. A prospectively frozen record. New outcomes must be frozen before inspection. Choosing a nicer setting, cheaper cost or endpoint from this panel does not reopen the verdict.

§9 · What survives

Under the registered primary conventions, in both long comparison windows, this published switch historically reduced the worst fall while keeping return within our tolerance of QQQ. That is the measured favourable result. It did not hold in every period: the risk bar held in only 1 of 2 later slices, and in 1 of 3 separate periods once the build years are counted. Settings, costs, period choice and concentrated gains constrain it. Which component of a portfolio can you actually test, and what evidence exists for the rest? That question survives even when the component does well.
