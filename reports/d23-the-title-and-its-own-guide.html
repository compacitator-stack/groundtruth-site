The third leg, crowding, is the heart of a rush of entrants, and it has no supply series in any source we could lawfully read: no count of Upwork’s freelancers, no proposals per job, and we did not scrape Upwork. What exists includes demand and pay. Money spent on AI-related work grew “more than 50%”, then “more than 40%”, then “more than 22%” year over year in Upwork’s last three quarters: still growing, but slowing. The definitions behind Upwork’s figures

The “AI video generation and editing (+329%)” figure is total freelancer earnings in that skill, on completed jobs with demand from the United States, 2025 compared with 2024: demand across everyone, not pay per person and not your odds. The “34% more per hour” figure compares freelancers doing AI work with those not incorporating AI at one time; it is not a trend. Upwork’s survey line, “Skilled freelancers now represent 38% of U.S. knowledge workers, up from 28% the prior year.” comes from a survey of US workers, not platform data. Active clients on Upwork went 851k (2023), 832k (2024), 785k (2025) and 763k (Q2 2026); its 10-K for 2025 gives the reason for 2025: “slower growth in acquisition of new clients as well as lower retention”.

The other two legs, in detail

The on-ramp. Upwork launched its own Model Context Protocol server in August 2026: its SEC-filed results release of 2026-08-10 lists the launch, “allowing clients and freelancers to direct AI agents to find talent, source opportunities, and help manage work”. Upwork’s release that day says a freelancer’s agent can “draft a tailored proposal” and that the server is available “at no additional cost”. Fulfillment by Amazon first appears in Amazon’s 10-K for 2006 and not in the one for 2005: it opened twelve years before the 2018 rush.

The house is paid whether or not you are hired. Connects are “virtual tokens required for talent to bid on projects and purchase ads products”; in 2023 Upwork “increased the number of Connects needed by talent to bid on projects”. Upwork’s take rate, the share it keeps of all money spent on its marketplace, rose from 13.8% (2022) to 15.4% (2023), 18.0% (2024), 18.7% (2025) and 19.6% (Q2 2026), and its filings credit recent rises to “ads and monetization products”; the take rate also carries fee changes, so its rise is not all Connects. “The Upwork filings we read don't say how much it makes from Connects”: none of the seven Upwork SEC filings we read (two 10-Ks, two 10-Qs, three SEC-filed results releases) sizes it. In 2018 Amazon’s Professional plan cost “$39.99 a month + additional selling fees”, sale or no sale.

Who earns from selling the method (beside the count). The method is given away; the video’s description carries an email sign-up, affiliate links and a mentoring offer whose price we did not read. A viewer who buys the Claude plan the video was filmed on pays Anthropic whether or not a client is ever won. Jungle Scout, which sells tools to Amazon sellers, published the survey of them that we read (fielded in November and December 2019).

Mechanisms that differ, in the presenter’s favour

No inventory to buy or dump; work made to order rather than a commodity listing; work with clients a freelancer brings to the platform carries “a low 5% service fee” (Direct Contracts, shown in the video); and an entrant can stop with a small sunk cost.

Base rates and research (report-only, except the 1.2 million new sellers, which our video says)

Amazon, 2018: “This year more than 1.2 million sellers joined all Amazon marketplaces”; since 2017, “3.3 million new third-party sellers have joined” while sellers with $100,000 or more in sales rose by about 180,000, and “Most of the new sellers don't become active” (Marketplace Pulse). Jungle Scout’s 2020 report says “86 % of Amazon sellers are profitable”, but its respondents were “1,046 experienced Amazon sellers who have more than a year of selling experience and at least one live product listing”: survivors by construction, self-reported, surveyed by a tool seller. We never apply that rate to Upwork.

Two working papers, context only, because their data end in 2023: on Upwork from January 2022 to April 2023, freelancers in writing-related occupations, compared with freelancers in less-affected occupations, saw monthly jobs fall 2% and monthly earnings 5.2% after ChatGPT (Hui, Reshef and Zhou). On one large platform from July 2021 to July 2023, job posts for writing and coding fell 20.86% more than posts for manual-intensive work, image-creation posts fell 17.01% relative to manual-intensive posts, and among employers who posted before and after, bids per automation-prone post rose 8.57% relative to manual-intensive posts (Demirci, Hannane and Zhu). Neither measures AI video ads, Shopify pages or 2025-26.
