Margin vs LEAPS — What Leverage Actually Costs

Episode C02 · report format v2 · lab run 2020-07-01 → 2026-06-22, pre-registration locked before any code ran before the run
VERDICT — THE NON-CALLABLE VERSION IS CORRECTLY PRICED, AND AT A SAFE RATIO YOU DO NOT NEED IT

Held at 1.33× for six years, the option construction costs +1.37% a year more than the margin loan on the index fund and +1.43% on the volatile name. That is a real price for removing a real risk. But at that ratio the margin book's worst equity ratio over the whole window was 75% against a 25% maintenance requirement — it was never called, and it was never close. Insurance, correctly priced, against something the construction had already excluded.

What we granted, and what we actually tested

Granted, not measured: that long-dated deep-in-the-money calls really do remove the margin call. They do. There is no loan behind them, so no broker can force the position closed. That half of the pitch is true and we are not testing it.

Tested: what it costs. We ran the same 1.33× exposure two ways over the same six years and charged both sides honestly — bought at the ask, marked at the bid every day, commission on both sides, dividends credited against the loan. 13 entries, 11 of them rolls.

What this report does NOT claim, pre-committed before the data: this is two tickers, not a class, and the volatile one is a single high-volatility cyclical rather than a category. The window is bull-dominated with one real bear in it. Taxes are excluded, and that runs against the option arm, because rolling realises a gain about every twelve months while a held margin position defers it. After tax the gap gets wider, not narrower.

§1 · The locked pre-registration

Committed 6beaa55 on before any code ran, before the probe was run — the wager you cannot move after seeing the cards.

The falsifiable question

Does the non-callable construction cost less than the margin loan it replaces, at a ratio a careful person would actually hold?

  • A yes looks like: the option arm's CAGR drag lands at or below 1 point a year.
  • A no looks like: the drag exceeds 2 points a year, and margin construction stands.
  • Ambiguous residue: between 1 and 2 points a year — situational, and only for ambitions above 1.33×. This is where the answer landed.

Our stated prior: the drag would be real but under a point

We were wrong, and wrong in the direction that made the product look better than it is. Both names came in above the band we guessed.

§3 · Was it the path? The stress tests

The margin arm's worst equity ratio over the whole six years was 75% on the index fund and 71% on the volatile name, against a 25% maintenance requirement. On the index fund that is where the book started — the cushion never narrowed at all.

This was not a kind window. At 1.33× and a 25% maintenance requirement the hinge is a 67% fall, and the deepest fall in the record was 37.8% intraday. The construction excluded the event. The weather never got a vote. The converse is just as true and it is ours to say: never called at this ratio is a statement about arithmetic, not about the future.

§4 · The controls

Buy-and-hold, unlevered, over the same window: QQQ · the index fund 19.76% · MU · the volatile name 70.38%. Realized beta lands where 1.33× says it should — margin 1.21 vs LEAPS 1.18 (QQQ) · margin 1.15 vs LEAPS 1.10 (MU), so the two arms really are carrying the same exposure and the comparison is like-for-like.

§6 · Their own terms

Two things run against us and we are stating them rather than glossing them.

Our strike rule is not their strike rule. We chose the largest listed strike at or below 70% of spot; the two educators whose lesson this prices choose by delta and aim for 70–80. Those are not the same contract. On the index fund, moving to an 80% strike adds a median of about 1.4 points a year in entry premium — so our figure is a floor on their version, not a price for it.

We rolled at six months; at least one creator teaches three. Different positions on the time-decay curve. Which is more expensive is something we did not measure and will not guess.

§8 · What would change our mind

  1. A window and a ratio where the margin book is force-sold and the option book is not. That is the one condition under which the drag stops being a fee and becomes a bargain, and our six years never produced one. Send it and we run it here first.
  2. A broker whose maintenance requirement does not rise in a crash. That would move this on the arithmetic alone — every threshold we quote is the generous one, because a margin agreement reserves the right to raise the requirement exactly when the price is testing it.
  3. The delta-selected version, priced. Ours is a floor on the strike they teach, not a price for it. Somebody running their rule and showing the premium is a better episode than this one.

§9 · What survives

A margin quote has two prices. The rate is on the screen. The second one — the fall that ends you — is in the maintenance schedule, your broker recomputes it nightly, and it moves with what you post. A threshold without its maintenance requirement is not a number.

And the thing worth carrying: correctly priced protection, bought against a risk your construction has already excluded, is still a cost. At 1.33× you are paying about a point and a half a year for a margin call that the arithmetic says cannot reach you.
