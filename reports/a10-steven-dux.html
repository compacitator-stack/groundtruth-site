Steven Dux Said His Own Edge Was Fading. We Measured It.

Episode A10 · report format v2 · lab run 2026-06-30 (market-cap probe) · 2026-08-16 01:02 UTC (decay probe), pre-registrations dated 2026-06-30 and 2026-08-15 — not hash-locked
VERDICT — ONE VERSION OF HIS RULE IS FALSE. THE OTHER WAS REAL IN 2021 AND IS GONE BY 2023 — AND HE SAID SO FIRST

He states the same mechanism two ways in two interviews, so we pre-registered and ran both. Sectioned by market cap it is false: across 76 survivorship-free parabolic runs drawn from 1,058 detected, the dollar volume at the top is not clustered inside a cap bucket, and the multiplier does not hold constant — it collapses from 121 to 18 to 6 as cap rises, which is the opposite of the stated shape. Sectioned by year it was real, and large. In 2021, a normally-dead micro-cap that printed $1 billion of volume in a day closed lower the next day 69.7% of the time with a median of -9.93%, against a 48.9% / +0.00% base rate in the same population and the same year — +20.7pp, on 234 events, measured from one closing price to the next. By 2023, at $200 million — the number he himself gives for that year — the same measurement returns +5.3pp and a median of -0.17%. He told his audience on camera that the number was falling. He was right. The correction we owe him is that it fell differently than described: what decayed was not the level, it was the effect. And one thing this page does not establish is whether any of it was ever available — a next-day close-to-close return is not a trade, and we have never been able to measure borrow or locate for this population.

What we granted, and what we actually tested

Granted, not measured: three things, none of them grudgingly. He makes money, and the record is better-attested than the genre norm — third-party verified by his own account, and he invites direct brokerage verification on camera. We did not audit it and we do not dispute it. His stated facts check out where we can check them: we pulled the three figures he gives from memory on camera and scored them against our survivorship-free daily store — 3 of 3 inside the stated tolerance. He says HPX traded “it only trade around the 1.4 million shares throughout the day”; the store says 1,431,196 shares, 2.2% off. He says it ran to $50; the high was $50.50. On a delisted ghost, three years later, from memory. And he named the decay himself, unprompted, on camera — which is the whole reason the second probe exists. Sources credited and linked: Steven Dux — first-green-day walkthrough (his own channel) · Humbled Trader — the statistics interview with Steven Dux.

Tested: the two stated formulations of one mechanism, each pre-registered before its run. Formulation one, from the first-green-day walkthrough: a parabolic micro-cap tops at roughly the same cumulative dollar volume for a given starting market cap. Formulation two, from the interview: a day’s volume times its average traded price crossing a ceiling means the stock falls the next day — and the ceiling fell from $1 billion in 2021 to $200 million in 2023. 1,058 detected runs → 76 usable on the first; 6,778 normally-illiquid tickers, 47 scored cells, every one of them against a same-year control arm on the second.

What this report does NOT claim, pre-committed before the data: that any of this was reachable. There is no entry, no exit, no friction, no borrow and no locate in a single number on this page — a next-day close-to-close return is not a trade, and we do not claim the effect was harvestable by anyone in any year. Nor does it claim anything about his execution, his sizing, or his discretion, which is the part he says repeatedly is where his money is made. Nor about the float-sectioned version of the rule, which is the one he may actually run and which is untestable in principle with any data we can obtain. Nor whether he can be copied. Nothing here measures that.

§1 · The pre-registrations — and what they are not

These two pre-registrations are dated, not hash-locked, and we are saying so. There are two of them because he states the mechanism two different ways in two different interviews, and each got its own frozen goalposts before its own run: 2026-06-30 and 2026-08-15. Both files say in their first line that they were locked before any result existed, and both runs are internally consistent with that. But no commit lock exists for either one. The later lab's pre-registration, its probe and its results all landed in a single commit, so version control cannot witness the ordering; the earlier lab is not in a repository at all. What stands behind the dates is a written attestation, and an attestation is not cryptographic evidence. Filling that gap with a hash that does not exist would be the exact move this channel was built to catch, so the gap is printed instead.

The falsifiable question

Two questions, one per formulation. Does the signal exist at all — conditional on a normally-illiquid micro-cap printing a daily dollar volume above the threshold he names, is the next day negative materially more often, and more deeply, than the matched base rate for the same population in the same year? And does the ceiling decay as he says — is the best-separating threshold in 2021 at least 3× the one in 2023, falling monotonically in between? Behind both, the older question the first probe asked: does the dollar volume at the top cluster inside a market-cap bucket, at a stable multiple of cap?

  • A yes looks like: the event arm beats its control arm by 10 points or more on the share of down-next-days in both 2021 and 2023 at his own stated levels, with a median at least 2 points below control in both — and, on the older probe, the middle-50% spread inside every powered cap bucket coming in under 1.00 with a stable multiplier.
  • A no looks like: either year failing either bar, or the multiplier moving more than 3× across cap buckets. Both are what happened, in different probes.
  • Ambiguous residue: exactly one condition of a pair landing marginal — pre-committed to be reported and leaned on, naming the artifact. The decay probe did not land here: it split cleanly, one question failing and the other passing.

Our stated prior: we leaned FALSE on the signal and called the decay a coin flip — and we were wrong about the most important year

The frozen prior read: lean FALSE on the signal (~70%), genuinely uncertain on the decay (~50%). The reasoning was that micro-cap parabolas mean-revert on average — but so does the control arm — so we expected a real but small gap failing the 10-point bar in both years. The verdict came out where we guessed and the mechanism did not. 2023 failed as predicted, at +5.3pp. 2021 did not: it cleared the bar twice over at +20.7pp. Being right about the headline for the wrong reason is still being wrong, and the direction of the error runs toward him, not away.

§3 · Was it the path? The stress tests

Was the 2021 result a handful of meme names? No. Dropping the five largest events in each year moves 2021 from +20.7pp to +21.0pp — it goes up — and leaves 2023 unchanged at +5.3pp (+5.35pp against +5.26pp before the drop). Neither year is carried by its tail.

Did the losers quietly delist before we could measure them? That would bias toward the claim, so we counted it. Rows with no next-day close: 0 of 234 in the 2021 cell and 0 of 789 in the 2023 cell.

The single cleanest diagnostic in the whole probe is the sign of a slope. In 2021 the gap grows as the threshold rises — +9.3pp at $50M → +12.8pp at $200M → +17.6pp at $500M → +19.6pp at $750M — which is exactly what a real supply-exhaustion ceiling should do: the bigger the day, the bigger the fade. In 2023 it does the opposite. It shrinks as the threshold rises and then turns negative — +7.4pp at $50M → +5.3pp at $200M → +3.3pp at $500M → -7.1pp at $750M. That slope inverted between the two years. It is the reason we read the result as an effect that died rather than a ceiling that moved. Every step above is a cell with at least 50 events in both years; the 2023 gap runs further negative above $750M, but on 23 events, which is under our own power floor and carries no verdict. It is in the ledger, marked not powered.

And on the older probe, the market-cap version fails the same way twice more. Restricted to the more recent half of the record (n = 67) the multiplier still spans 6.4× across cap buckets; with the three largest names removed (n = 73) it still spans 6.6×, against a pre-registered bar of 3. The single most extreme row in that ledger is BMNR: a $9M company that traded $10.3 billion on the way to its top, for a multiplier of 1,137.6 against a bucket median of 121.3. A rule that says “roughly the same dollar amount” has to survive that row, and it does not.

§4 · The controls

Every event number on this page is scored against a control arm, and the control arm is the reason the numbers mean anything. It is every ticker-day in the same population, in the same year, that did not cross the threshold. Without it this probe would measure micro-cap drift and call it a signal.

The control arm is remarkably stable: 2021 48.9% down-next-days on 599,363 non-crossing ticker-days, drawn from a population of 3,117 names and 599,979 ticker-days · 2022 48.6% down-next-days on 690,823 non-crossing ticker-days, drawn from a population of 3,271 names and 691,419 ticker-days · 2023 48.7% down-next-days on 643,528 non-crossing ticker-days, drawn from a population of 3,197 names and 647,472 ticker-days · 2024 50.0% down-next-days on 610,798 non-crossing ticker-days, drawn from a population of 2,956 names and 612,744 ticker-days · 2025 50.5% down-next-days on 600,756 non-crossing ticker-days, drawn from a population of 2,921 names and 603,225 ticker-days. A coin flip, every year, and a median next-day return that never lands further than 0.05 points from zero in any of them. That is the line the event arm has to beat, and in 2021 it beat it by +20.7pp.

On the market-cap probe the control is a different shape: a bootstrap confidence interval on each bucket median. Those intervals are wide, and the width is the finding — a claim that parabolas top at roughly the same dollar amount needs a narrow one. The middle-50% spread over the median came in at 1.56 · 1.08 · 1.89 against a pre-registered bar of 1.00.

§5 · The best objection, pre-registered and answered

“You tested the market-cap version. I trade the float version. My dollar block is trapped shares times price — that is float, not market cap, and you just told your audience you cannot measure float.”

That objection is correct, and we are conceding it rather than answering it. The house rule here is that a steelman gets answered with a run, not a caveat. This one cannot be, and saying so plainly is the finding: company filings publish no share-float tag, so the float-sectioned version of his rule is untestable in principle with any data we can obtain. Settling it needs a float-data vendor, and that is the first item in §8.

The second objection is ours, against ourselves. 2023’s best-separating threshold is $50M — which is the bottom cell of our own sweep grid. An estimator that lands on its own boundary has run out of road. So the honest reading is not that the ceiling relocated downward to a precise new level; it is that by 2023 there is no threshold effect left to locate and the sweep returned its least-bad cell. That is why this report publishes the ladder and the slope and never a decay multiple: the multiple is an artifact of where we put the floor of the grid, and a number we cannot defend is a number we do not print.

The third is the one that matters most and it has no answer here at all. Nothing above is a trade. There is no entry, no exit, no slippage, no commission, no borrow fee and no locate in any figure on this page. From three unrelated short-side studies of our own, the binding constraint on this kind of position was never the borrow fee — it measured near 1% of gross on an intraday hold — it was whether a locate existed at all, and in all three the apparent edge sat entirely inside the part of the market that could not be borrowed. Those are measurements about our own strategies, not about his, and they carry no verdict on him. Whether the 2021 effect was ever available to anybody is not established on this page.

§8 · What would change our mind

  1. Given a real share-float series for micro-caps, we re-run the identical probe sectioned by float instead of market cap. If the dollar volume at the top clusters against float and its multiple is stable across float buckets, his mechanism is right and our sectioning was wrong. The single row that would flip it is already in our ledger: BMNR, a $9M company that traded $10.3 billion. Send us the data and we run it here first.
  2. A locate-availability feed. This is the bounty we most want and the one we have never been able to buy. Everything anyone might say about whether the 2021 effect was reachable — ours included — is inference until a series exists showing what was actually borrowable, on which day, at what size. Until then this page reports a price regularity and stops there.
  3. A denser threshold grid below $50M. Our 2023 estimate sits on the floor of the grid, so the magnitude of the decay is genuinely unresolved. Our reading is that the effect died rather than the ceiling relocating, and that reading is falsifiable: if a finer grid under $50M shows the gap climbing again, we were wrong about which of the two happened.
  4. Minute bars for the illiquid tail. Our intraday universe is selected on lifetime median dollar volume, which excludes a pump’s own population by construction. With that data the entire first-green-day half of his method becomes testable, and it is currently untested rather than refuted.
  5. A reconstruction that contradicts this grid. Every cell is in the CSV above. If you rebuild the population, the control arm or the event definition and get a different answer, publish it and we will link it from this page.

§9 · What survives

A rule with a number in it has an expiry date, and almost nobody who sells one tells you that. He did. He stood in an interview and said the level had fallen from $1 billion to $200 million in two years. We went and measured it, and the direction he gave was right. The part he did not say — possibly did not know — is that the level was not the thing that was decaying. The effect was. In 2021 the bigger the day, the bigger the next-day fade; by 2023 that relationship had inverted, and a buyer handed a new number would be handed a live-looking parameter for a mechanism that had gone quiet.

So the transferable question is not “what is the number now.” It is “compared to what.” 69.7% of those days closed lower, which sounds like a lot until you learn that 48.9% of all days in that population closed lower too. The gap is the claim. Everything else is the weather.

And the honest end of it: we measured a price regularity, not an opportunity. Whether anyone could have stood in front of it — found the borrow, got the locate, sized it without moving it, survived the left tail he himself describes — is a question this page does not answer and did not try to. That is the reopen bounty, and it is still open.
